Overview

Hearts is played with a standard deck of 52 cards.  Cards are valued Ace high down to 2 low.  Each heart is worth 1 point and the queen of spades is worth 13.  Optionally, the Jack of Diamonds can be scored as negative 10 points.  The object of the game is to avoid scoring points and the game is usually over when one player reaches 100 points.  If there is a tie for the least number of points, play continues until there is a clear winner.

Deal and Passing

All cards from the deck are dealt clockwise, one at a time to each player until all cards are dealt.  This results in each player having 13 cards in their hand.  On the first hand of the game, each player selects 3 cards and passes them to the player to their left.  On the second hand, each player selects 3 cards and passes them to the player to their right.  On the third hand, each player selects 3 cards and passes them to the player across.  On the fourth hand, no cards are passed.  The cycle then repeats until the game is over.

As a game option, you can select how the cards will be passed : only pass cards to the left, pass cards left then right, pass cards left, right, across, then hold, or don't pass cards.

Playing

Typically, the player with the 2 of Clubs leads the first trick of each hand.  However, as a game option, you can select to always have the player left of the dealer start the first trick.  The first dealer of each game will always be the player across from your hand.  The deal is then passed clockwise to the next player after each hand.

After the first card of a trick is played, play continues clockwise.  Each player must follow suit with the lead card whenever possible.  If the player cannot follow suit, they can then play a point card or other suit.  Once a heart is played on a trick, hearts are said to be 'broken' and can then be lead in the following tricks.  The highest value card that followed suit wins the trick and any points are scored to that player.  The top-left corner of your screen displays the score for the current hand. 'A' is the player Across from you; 'R' is the player to your right; 'L' is the player to your left; and 'Y' is You.  The winner of the trick then leads the next trick. Play continues until all 13 tricks are played.

Game Options

From the menu, select 'Options' to set the game's appearance, rules, and difficulty.

Display Options:
Select any of the 5 card styles or the '?' card to have the card style randomly selected before each game.

Check the box 'Tap before taking trick' if you want the program to wait for a tap before removing the cards.

'Delay between cards played' sets how long the program delays between each card played on the board.

Rule Options:
Select which player begins each hand. Either the player left of the dealer or the player holding the 2 of Clubs.   The player holding the 2 of Clubs is the most common way Hearts is played today.

Pass Cards 'L', 'L-R', 'L-R-A-H', 'don't' indicates the direction cards are to be passed ( L=Left, R=Right, A=Across, H=Hold ). This option takes effect with the next hand played or 'New Game'.

Hearts must be broken before led should be checked if you want to prevent hearts from being led.  Hearts is considered broken after a heart has been played or 'painted' to a trick.

Queen of Spades breaks hearts should be checked if you want hearts to be considered broken after the Queen of Spades has been played.

Points allowed on first trick should be checked if you want to allow a point card to be played on the first trick of a hand.

J. Diamonds Counts -10 should be checked if you want the Jack of Diamonds to be counted as -10 points.

J.D. must be won to shoot moon.  This option should be checked if you are counting the Jack of Diamonds as -10 points and you want to require a player to also win the Jack of Diamonds before considered shooting the moon .

Shooting Sun worth 52 pts should be checked if you want to make shooting the sun worth 52 pts.  Shooting the Sun is when a player not only wins all points in a hand but, also every trick.

Difficulty Options:
The different levels indicate how thorough your opponents will be in deciding which cards to play and in trying to prevent you from shooting the moon.  Try all levels for a change in how your opponents play.
