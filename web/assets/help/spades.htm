Overview and Object

Spades is played with a standard deck of 52 cards.  Cards are valued Ace high down to 2 low and Spades are considered a trump suit.  Spades can be played either as individuals or with partners.  When partner play is selected, your partner is the player across from you and your team is 'We'.  Your opponents are left and right of your hand and they are 'They'.  When played as individuals, you can select to play with 2 or 3 opponents.

The object of Spades is to win points which are earned by meeting you or your team's bid placed at the beginning of each hand.  You can also lose points by falling short of your bids or by taking too many tricks beyond your bid.  The game is typically over when a team or individual reaches 500 points.

Deal

All cards from the deck are dealt clockwise, one at a time to each player until all cards are dealt.  When playing as partners or individuals with 3 opponents, this results in each player having 13 cards in their hand.  When playing as individuals and 2 opponents, each player is dealt 17 cards and the last card remains in the deck.

Play and Bid

Before play begins, you are asked to enter a bid.  This bid is the number of tricks you think you can win with your hand.  When playing with partners, your bid is combined with 'Across' to make up your team's bid.  If your team wins the number of tricks bid, you win 10 points times your bid plus 1 point for each overtrick -- or 'bag'.  For every 10 bags accumulated, your team loses 100 points.  Therefore, it is important to try and meet your bid as closely as possible.

Entering a bid of 'nil' indicates that you are not going to win any tricks.  If you are successful with a nil bid, your team earns 50 points.  If you fail and win 1 or more tricks, your team loses 50 points.  When bidding blind nil, the point value is 100.  Blind nil is available if your team has a score less than or equal to -100 points.

The eldest hand ( player left of the dealer ) leads the first trick of each hand.  The first dealer of a game is 'Across' and the deal is passed clockwise before each hand.  When playing as individuals, you may also set the game such that the player with the 2 of clubs leads each hand.

After the first card of a trick is played, play continues clockwise.  Each player must follow suit with the lead card whenever possible.  If the player cannot follow suit, they can then play any suit.  Once a spade is played on a trick, spades are said to be 'broken' and can then be lead in the following tricks.  If a spade is played on a trick, the highest spade card takes the trick since spades are the trump suit.  Otherwise, the highest card following suit is the winner.

Scoring

If your combined bid is met or exceeded, your score is 10 times your bid plus one point for every trick taken above your bid.  Overtricks are also called 'bags' and 100 points will be deducted from your score each time your team reaches 10 bags.  If your combined bid is not met, your score for this hand is -10 times your bid.

If you or your partner bids nil, any overtricks from that player will not be counted toward fulfilling your team's bid.  That is if you bid 0 and take 1, your partner bids 3 and takes 2, your overtrick will not be counted to fulfill the combined contract.  Successfully completing a nil bid gives your team 50 pts and failing a nil bid results in -50 pts.  Blind nil doubles the point value.  Blind nils can only be entered once your team's score is less than or equal to -100 points ( or other value defined in the rule options ).

Once a team reaches the game ending score ( default is 500 pts), that team is declared the winner.

Game Options

Under display options, select the card style you would like to use or the '?' card to have the style randomly selected before each game.  Checking the 'Tap before taking trick' button will require you to tap the screen before the cards are removed at the end of each trick.  'Delay between cards played' indicates how long the program waits before the next player plays a card.

Under rule options, select how many points are necessary to win the game : 300, 500, or 1000.  You may also set the score that is necessary before a blind nil bid can be placed.  The default for the blind nil option is -100 pts.   You may also select if you would like to play as partners or individuals.  When playing a partner game, your partner is always the player across from your hand.  When playing as individuals, you will be able to select how many opponents you will have -- 2 or 3.  When playing with 2 opponents, each of the 3 players will be dealt 17 cards.   The last card of the deck is not dealt.  With individual play, you can either select that the player holding the 2 of clubs leads or the player left of the dealer leads each hand.  When the 2 of clubs leads and this card happens to be the one not dealt, the player holding the 3 of clubs will lead.

Under bidding options, select how you would like the computer players to bid.  Normal uses the built-in algorithm to determine how many tricks each player thinks they can win.  Normal + 1 adds 1 to the normal bid excluding nil bids.   Spades will bid the same as the number of spades in each hand.  Face cards will bid the number of face cards ( excluding Aces ) in each hand.  Reverse will bid the same as the number of diamonds in each hand.  When Spades, Face Cards, or Reverse is selected, your bid will default to that number but, can be overriden before the hand starts.
